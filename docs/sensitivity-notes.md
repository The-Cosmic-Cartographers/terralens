# Serial correlation and sensitivity notes

Generated by `python -m analysis.sensitivity` from the processed series. Every number below comes from the same code path that writes `data/results/`, so this page cannot drift from the app.

## Method-box text (app)

> **How the trend is measured.** Each month is compared with its 1991–2020 normal for that calendar month, and the seasonal Mann–Kendall test asks whether the resulting anomalies rise or fall consistently through the record. Because consecutive months are not independent, the test's variance is corrected with the Hamed–Rao method using the lag-1 autocorrelation of the residuals; without it, a slow-moving series would look more certain than it is. The slope is Sen's slope — the median of all pairwise slopes — reported per decade with a 95% confidence interval.
>
> **What we check.** The same test on annual means is shown as a sensitivity check: if the monthly and annual results disagree, the row is fragile and we say so. Every verdict uses the Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted p-value for its family (a region's five rows, or one variable across regions), so the chance of a false positive does not grow with the number of rows on screen.
>
> **What a verdict means.** *Significant trend* means the adjusted p-value is below 0.05. *Weak evidence* is 0.05–0.10 and is not a claim. *No clear trend* means the record cannot show one — not that nothing changed. *Not enough data* means the coverage rules failed before any test ran, and no slope or p-value is reported at all.

## Limitations worth stating out loud

* **Reanalysis is not observation.** MERRA-2 is a model-plus-assimilation product; a trend in them is a trend in the assimilated system, not a thermometer.
* **The p-value depends on the window.** The period table above exists because a verdict can change with the start and end years, and pretending otherwise would be the single easiest way to mislead a reader.
* **"No clear trend" is not "no change".** With 21–45 years and this much year-to-year noise, many real changes are simply not detectable yet; the detectability figure says how long a record would be needed.
* **One change point at most.** Pettitt finds a single level shift and is conservative on short records; it is a diagnostic, never a verdict.
* **No attribution.** Nothing here says why a trend exists, and no result should be worded as if it did.
